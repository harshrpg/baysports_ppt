Baysports Payments and Invoice Management Design

This document defines how to extend the existing Baysports booking system with a simple customer payment experience and stronger back-office financial controls. It covers the business problem, the existing technical foundations, proposed workflows, integration boundaries, financial records, security, compliance, delivery, and acceptance criteria.

The recommended solution keeps booking operations in the current application. It adds a backend-controlled payments capability, a small customer portal, and a finance workspace. Card payments and open banking use specialist providers. Brisk remains the proposed invoice system, subject to confirming a supported integration.

Evidence distinction: “Existing” below means supported by inspected code or your instructions. “Proposed” describes the design to implement. Items requiring further verification are identified explicitly. All diagrams describe proposed behaviour unless labelled otherwise.

1. The business problem is the gap between a booking being organised and its money being collected and accounted for.

The existing system manages school visits. The next stage must make it easy to determine:

  • What has the school agreed to purchase?
  • What must it pay now, and what becomes due later?
  • Which invoices have been issued?
  • Has payment actually succeeded?
  • Has the payment been recorded in Brisk?
  • Has the provider paid the money into Baysports’ bank?
  • What happens when a school changes numbers, cancels, overpays, or needs a refund?

These questions currently risk being answered through different records: booking fields, Brisk, bank statements, emails, and manual notes.

The operational problem is therefore broader than adding a “Pay” button. The system needs a consistent financial history, with clear ownership of each stage.

Figure 1 — The financial coordination problem described in the meeting notes. This is a business-process representation, not a claim that every manual step is implemented in code.

The intended outcome is that customers understand what to pay, staff can resolve exceptions quickly, and accountants can trace every financial movement back to a booking and its documents.

3. The extension has three product areas.

Area Included capability
Customer view View booking, see payment schedule, pay, download documents, and contact the office.
Back-office finance Payment controls, invoices, credit notes, refunds, reconciliation, reminders, and exceptions.

Existing booking, capacity, package, and resource management remain the operational foundation.

Customer amendments should initially be requests to the office. Automatically changing dates, headcounts, packages, or cancellation outcomes would involve existing booking rules and deserves a separate implementation decision.

The customer interface must never submit an amount that the backend accepts without checking. It requests an authorised action; the backend determines the amount and permitted payment options.

6. Customer access must be separate from staff access.

The portal should use an invitation tied to an identified booking contact or school finance contact.

The access record should specify:

  • Who may access the booking.
  • Which booking or explicitly authorised group of bookings they may access.
  • Whether they can view documents, pay, or manage invitations.
  • When access expires or is revoked.
  • Which staff member or approved process granted access.

School-email-domain membership is not sufficient evidence of entitlement to every booking for that school.

A suitable flow is:

Figure 5 — Proposed customer authorisation flow.

Customer sessions should be protected against guessing, replay, excessive requests, and cross-booking access. Every document download and payment action requires backend authorisation.

Provider callbacks require their own authentication mechanism, such as verified signatures. They should not become generally trusted merely because they bypass an interactive staff login.

7. The financial model must separate commercial value, documents, collection, and settlement.

Record Purpose
Accepted price version Preserves the agreed booking value and terms.
Instalment Defines how much is due and when.
Invoice Records an issued financial charge.
Credit note Reduces or corrects an issued charge.
Payment request Offers a controlled opportunity to pay.
Collection attempt Tracks an individual card or bank-payment attempt.
Confirmed payment Records money recognised as received from the customer.
Payment allocation Applies a payment against an invoice or obligation.
Refund Tracks money being returned.
Settlement Tracks provider payout and bank reconciliation.
Integration operation Tracks communication with Brisk or another provider.

An instalment divides an obligation. It must not create a second charge.

A credit note changes the amount charged. A refund returns money. Customer credit is an available balance. These concepts must remain distinct.

Figure 6 — Conceptual financial relationships. This is not a final database schema. Manual payments can exist without a provider collection attempt.

Store monetary amounts in integer minor units, such as euro cents. Preserve the currency, external identifiers, timestamps, and originating payer.

For a single billing account and currency:

Net invoiced obligation
    = issued invoice charges − issued credit notes

Net confirmed collection
    = confirmed payments − successful refunds or returns

Account position
    = net invoiced obligation − net confirmed collection

Invoice balances use allocations, with unapplied money tracked separately. A payment awaiting confirmation does not count as received. A refund awaiting confirmation reserves funds but does not pretend they have already left.

The remaining booking balance may differ from the invoiced amount currently due, particularly when the final invoice has not yet been issued. The backend must expose these as separate values.

8. The deposit and balance workflow must avoid duplicate invoicing.

For an illustrative €2,000 booking with a €500 deposit:

Model Invoice behaviour Payment behaviour
Full invoice with instalments Issue €2,000 once. Collect €500, then €1,500 against it.
Staged invoices Issue €500, then an incremental €1,500. Collect each stage against its invoice.

The accountant should select the appropriate model and tax treatment. The app must not issue a €500 deposit charge followed by an additional €2,000 charge for the same €2,000 booking.

Revenue states that VAT generally becomes chargeable when an advance payment is received. Invoice timing therefore needs an accounting rule rather than being driven only by booking status. Revenue deposits, Advance-payment invoice requirements

Figure 7 — Proposed collection lifecycle.

Booking confirmation should remain an existing backend workflow. If the business later chooses “confirm automatically when deposit is received,” the payment event must call that workflow and respect its capacity and validation rules.

Payment success must never be discarded because operational confirmation subsequently fails. That situation requires a visible exception and a business resolution.

9. Card payments require a verified backend outcome.

The proposed card integration uses hosted checkout. Baysports controls the booking amount and payment purpose; the provider handles the card-entry experience and applicable authentication.

Figure 8 — Card payment sequence.

The redirect improves the customer experience; provider evidence determines the financial outcome.

Stripe documents duplicate events, asynchronous webhook handling, signature verification, and idempotent requests. The implementation should expect retries and use database uniqueness and transaction controls to prevent duplicate financial effects. Stripe webhooks, Stripe idempotent requests

The backend should not keep a database transaction open while waiting for a remote provider call. It should persist intent, make the remote request, and reconcile uncertain outcomes safely.

10. Open banking needs a longer-lived pending workflow.

Open banking is not currently connected, according to your correction.

TrueLayer is a candidate because it documents Irish payment links suitable for invoices, deposits, and instalments. Its current provider documentation distinguishes retail, business, and corporate banking capabilities. Coverage for Baysports’ actual school accounts and multiple-authoriser arrangements still needs confirmation. TrueLayer payment links, Bank-provider capabilities

Figure 9 — Open-banking flow, including delayed confirmation and expired links.

The selected integration must define what constitutes sufficient receipt evidence. TrueLayer distinguishes authorisation, execution, and settlement; settlement into a provider merchant account is different from a direct-to-bank arrangement. TrueLayer integration checklist

A link expiring does not mean an existing payment failed.

Where a school cannot complete Pay by Bank, the portal should still offer an invoice and bank-transfer instructions. Staff should recognise a manual transfer only after verifying receipt, not simply because the customer uploads a screenshot.

11. Payment attempts need explicit states.

Figure 10 — Proposed collection-attempt state machine.

Refunds, disputes, and returns should be separate financial events linked to the original payment. They should not erase the historical fact that the original payment occurred.

Independent state Example
Booking Confirmed
Collection attempt Confirmed
Invoice balance Partly paid
Brisk synchronisation Needs attention

All five can be true simultaneously.

12. Brisk integration needs an explicit capability decision.

Brisk documents invoice payments, allocation amounts, references, and unapplied money. It also documents imports for customers, invoices, quotes, credit notes, and items. Payment imports are not listed. Brisk payments, Brisk imports

A public API or webhook contract was not verified. Brisk’s homepage also advertises payment gateways while stating that online card processing is available only in the US and Canada. That uncertainty must be resolved before relying on native Irish checkout. Brisk product information

Figure 11 — Invoice integration with an explicit manual fallback.

Before committing to automation, confirm:

  • Create and retrieve invoices and credit notes.
  • Retrieve invoice PDFs.
  • Record and reverse payment allocations.
  • Stable identifiers and supported duplicate-prevention mechanisms.
  • Notifications or a reliable reconciliation API.
  • Authentication, access limits, and supported Irish account configuration.
  • Data export, retention, and processing arrangements.

If Brisk cannot support the required automation, the business must choose between a controlled manual handoff and another invoice system. Undocumented browser automation should not become the core financial integration.

A customer payment remains received even if Brisk is unavailable. The customer should not be asked to pay again because accounting synchronisation failed.

13. Amendments, credits, and refunds need a controlled sequence.

A school may reduce numbers after paying a deposit, change food quantities, cancel, or request a goodwill adjustment.

The backend must first determine the revised commercial position under the accepted policy. It then determines the required document correction and any money to return.

Figure 12 — Amendment and refund workflow.

Worked example:

Stage Net invoiced charge Net money retained Customer position
€2,000 booking fully paid €2,000 €2,000 Balanced
Approved €400 reduction €1,600 €2,000 €400 credit
€250 refund succeeds €1,600 €1,750 €150 credit

Retaining the €150 as credit requires the applicable business/customer agreement. It is not an automatic substitute for a refund entitlement.

Two staff members must not be able to refund the same available amount simultaneously. Pending refund reservations should reduce the amount available for further refund requests.

A timeout after submitting a refund means “outcome unknown,” not “safe to submit a new refund.”

14. Reconciliation must connect customer payments to the money in the bank.

There are three separate checks:

  1. Payment against the customer’s obligation.
  2. Payment against the accounting record.
  3. Provider settlement against the bank statement.

Figure 13 — Reconciliation model.

For example, a €500 standard EEA card payment at Stripe’s published rate costs €7.75, leaving €492.25 before other applicable adjustments. The customer has paid €500, not €492.25. The €7.75 is a processing cost, not customer debt. Stripe Ireland pricing

The back office should show exceptions such as:

  • Money received without a recognised booking.
  • A recognised payment not yet recorded in Brisk.
  • A provider payout missing from the bank.
  • A bank receipt with an ambiguous reference.
  • A duplicate receipt or excess payment.
  • A refund with an uncertain outcome.

Each exception needs an owner, age, explanation, evidence, and next action.

16. Notifications should reuse Postmark and have durable delivery records.

The existing email integration should be extended rather than replaced solely for this project.

Payment messages include:

  • Payment invitation.
  • Deposit or balance reminder.
  • Payment received.
  • Payment failed or unresolved.
  • Invoice available.
  • Refund submitted and refund completed.
  • Internal reconciliation or integration exception.

Before sending a reminder, the backend should recheck:

  • The instalment remains unpaid.
  • The due date is still applicable.
  • No payment is awaiting resolution.
  • The booking has not entered a workflow that pauses collection.
  • The intended recipient still has the relevant contact role.
  • An equivalent reminder has not already been sent.

A financial transaction should commit its records and follow-up work together. A background worker then sends emails or performs accounting synchronisation.

Email failure must not roll back a confirmed payment. It should create a retryable notification issue.

Only one system should own each reminder schedule. Leaving both Brisk and Baysports independently chasing the same invoice risks duplicate or contradictory messages.

Payments received
Remaining booking balance

Commands should carry suitable idempotency and version information. If the booking amount changes while a customer has an old payment page open, the backend should return the current position and require an updated payment action.

No API should allow a customer to set a booking’s paid status or submit arbitrary invoice totals.

18. Migration must preserve historical evidence without inventing transactions.

The existing BookingPayment fields need a reconciliation-led migration.

In particular, it is not yet verified whether amountPaidCents includes a deposit or represents a separate amount. Adding it to depositAmountCents without inspecting the import and update logic could double-count historical money.

The migration process should:

  1. Inspect import mappings and existing update behaviour.
  2. Identify source invoice references and payment evidence.
  3. Classify records as verified, partially verified, or requiring review.
  4. Create traceable opening records only where the meaning is established.
  5. Preserve original values and notes as provenance.
  6. Reconcile opening balances with Brisk and bank/accountant records.
  7. Enable automated collection only for reviewed balances.

Historical dates and notes must not be converted into fictitious provider transaction IDs.

During rollout, define which model owns current balances. Avoid a permanent arrangement where the old summary and new transaction records are independently editable.

19. Security and compliance create concrete implementation requirements.

The inspected backend Dockerfile accepts database, Supabase service-role, and Postmark credentials as build arguments and places them into image environment variables. The staging workflow supplies these values during image creation. Payment credentials should use secure runtime injection, and the existing handling should be addressed before payment launch. This is an observed configuration pattern, not evidence that credentials have been stolen. Backend Dockerfile, Staging deployment

AWS documents supported mechanisms for supplying sensitive information to ECS containers. AWS ECS sensitive data

Area Design requirement
Staff security Least privilege, strong authentication for finance actions, and audited access changes
Customer security Booking-scoped access, protected sessions, revocable invitations, and request throttling
Payment security Hosted collection, verified callbacks, no card numbers or CVCs in logs or records
Financial integrity Recorded corrections, controlled refunds, duplicate prevention, and reconciliation
Data protection Necessary contact/billing information only; keep pupil health data out of payment screens
Document access Authorised downloads and retention appropriate to the document category
Operational recovery Durable work queues, observable failures, backups, and tested restoration

For Irish tax records, Revenue generally requires relevant business records to be retained for six years, with applicable exceptions. That does not justify keeping every personal-data field for the same period. Revenue recordkeeping

Invoice and credit-note fields, deposit treatment, activity/food VAT treatment, and cancellation accounting need accountant approval. Revenue invoice requirements, Revenue credit notes

Privacy responsibilities should cover lawful bases, access, processor arrangements, retention, and international transfers. Payment-provider roles may differ from ordinary hosting-provider roles and should be documented accordingly. DPC organisational guidance

Hosted checkout reduces PCI scope but does not automatically remove merchant obligations. The exact assessment should be confirmed with the acquirer. PCI SSC guidance

School organisational contracts and individual consumer purchases may have different legal treatment. Fixed-date leisure exceptions do not remove all service remedies or unfair-term controls. Refund capability should remain available even for packages described as non-refundable. CCPC service obligations

Assess the customer portal’s accessibility obligations, including applicability and exemptions under the European Accessibility Act. Keyboard navigation, readable errors, focus visibility, and accessible forms should be product requirements regardless. CCPC accessibility guidance

Rollout should initially cover a reviewed set of bookings. Importing every historical balance and immediately enabling reminders would introduce avoidable collection errors.

If a provider fails during operation, the app should retain its financial records, show the limitation, and queue appropriate recovery. Stopping new payment initiation must not stop processing previously initiated payments.

Business success should be measured through manual touches per booking, overdue receivables, time to resolve unmatched payments, reminder-related support issues, and the age of accounting-sync exceptions. Establish the baseline before setting improvement targets.

  • Brisk’s supported API capabilities and Irish account support.
  • The accountant-approved invoice and deposit model.
  • Deposit percentages, due dates, cancellation terms, and refund authority.
  • Open-banking coverage for representative school accounts.
  • The meaning and quality of historical payment fields.
  • Staff permissions, recovery targets, and support responsibilities.
